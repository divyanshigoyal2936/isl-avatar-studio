# Indian Sign Language (ISL) Grammar & Morphology Guide

## Key Principles of ISL Syntax

Indian Sign Language (ISL) is a distinct visual-spatial natural language with its own grammar, separate from spoken Indian languages and English.

### 1. Word Order: Subject - Object - Verb (SOV)
Unlike English (Subject - Verb - Object), ISL typically follows SOV or Topic-Comment structure:
- *English*: "The doctor is examining the patient."
- *ISL Gloss*: `DOCTOR PATIENT EXAMINE`

### 2. Time Marker First
Temporal reference in ISL is always established at the beginning of the signing space before actions occur:
- *English*: "I will not go to school today."
- *ISL Gloss*: `TODAY ME SCHOOL GO NO`

### 3. Interrogative (Question) Words at the End
Wh-question signs (`WHAT`, `WHERE`, `HOW`, `WHO`, `WHEN`) appear at the end of the clause, accompanied by non-manual facial markers:
- *English*: "Where is the hospital?"
- *ISL Gloss*: `HOSPITAL WHERE` (with furrowed eyebrows and head tilt)

### 4. Non-Manual Grammatical Markers (NMMs)
Facial expressions, eyebrow postures, head movements, and mouthing are integral grammatical elements, not optional emotion:
- **Wh-Questions**: Furrowed eyebrows, tilted head.
- **Yes/No Questions**: Raised eyebrows, forward head tilt.
- **Negation**: Sentence-final `NO` sign accompanied by a head shake.
- **Affirmation**: Head nod synchronized with sign hold.

### 5. Omission of Copulas & Function Words
ISL morphology does not use spoken language copulas ("is", "are", "am", "was") or grammatical articles ("a", "an", "the").
